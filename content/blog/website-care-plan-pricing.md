Recurring revenue is what turns a project-based web agency into a stable business. A website care plan — a monthly fee for keeping a client's site healthy — is the most natural way to get it. The hard parts are deciding what to include, what to charge, and making sure clients still see the value in month eleven.

## What clients are actually buying

Clients do not buy “maintenance”. They buy the absence of problems and the feeling that someone competent is responsible. Structure the plan around outcomes they understand:

- **The site stays online** — and if it goes down, you know before they do.
- **It stays secure** — updates, backups, valid SSL certificate.
- **Nothing expires** — domain and certificate renewals are tracked.
- **Small changes get done** — a bucket of minutes for content edits.
- **They can see it working** — a monthly report and a status page.

## A simple three-tier structure

| Tier | Typical contents |
|---|---|
| Essential | Uptime, SSL and domain monitoring · monthly report · backups · core updates |
| Business | Everything in Essential · plugin/theme updates · 30–60 min of changes · priority response |
| Growth | Everything in Business · performance reviews · 2–3 hours of changes · quarterly strategy call |

Three tiers work because most clients pick the middle one. Make the middle tier the one you most want to sell.

## Setting the price

Start from your costs and the value, not from what competitors charge:

1. **Time.** Estimate realistic monthly hours per tier, including the unglamorous bits — checking updates, answering emails, reading alerts.
2. **Tools.** Monitoring, backups, security scanning and hosting add a small per-site cost. Monitoring tools priced per account rather than per site make this almost negligible at scale.
3. **Risk.** You are taking on responsibility. Price for the occasional bad month.
4. **Value.** For a business that takes bookings or orders online, a single day of downtime can cost more than a year of the plan. Say so.

## Make the value visible every month

The biggest threat to a care plan is not a competitor — it is the client forgetting what they get. Send an automated monthly report under your brand with uptime, response times, incidents handled and upcoming renewals. Even in a quiet month, it shows the work is being done.

## Handling the “we never have problems” objection

That is the point. Show the report: the outages caught, the certificate renewed before it expired, the domain renewal flagged a month early. Quiet months are what a good plan looks like.

## Start with existing clients

Your easiest care-plan customers are the clients whose sites you already built. Offer the plan at handover for new projects, and to past clients with a short email: what you will monitor, what they will receive monthly, and the price. A short, specific offer converts far better than a long PDF.

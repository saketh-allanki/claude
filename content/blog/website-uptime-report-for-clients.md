When website maintenance goes well, nothing happens. The site stays up, the certificate renews, the domain never lapses. That is exactly what the client is paying for — and exactly why they start to wonder what they are paying for.

A short monthly report fixes that. It makes invisible work visible, gives you a reason to be in the client's inbox every month, and surfaces natural upsell conversations. Here is what to put in it.

## What a good client report includes

Keep it to one screen. Clients do not read long reports; they glance at a headline number and look for anything red.

### 1. A headline: overall uptime

One number, large: **99.97% uptime in September**. If it is above 99.9%, the client is reassured in one second.

### 2. Per-site summary

For each site or service you look after:

| Site | Uptime | Avg. response | Incidents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main website | 99.98% | 410 ms | 0 |
| Online booking | 99.91% | 620 ms | 1 |

Response time matters because slow pages lose visitors and rankings. Showing it every month makes performance work easy to justify.

### 3. Incidents — and what was done

List every outage with its start time and duration. If you have a sentence on the cause and the fix, add it. An incident resolved in 12 minutes is a story about your responsiveness, not a failure.

### 4. Upcoming renewals

List SSL certificates and domains expiring in the next 30–60 days. This is the single most underrated section: it shows foresight, prevents the most embarrassing outages, and often leads to a conversation about renewing for multiple years or consolidating domains.

### 5. A link to a live status page

A public status page lets the client check status any time without emailing you.

## A simple template

> **Website report — September**
>
> Your websites were online **99.97%** of the time in September. We monitored 3 sites around the clock.
>
> **Incidents:** Online booking was unavailable for 12 minutes on 14 Sept due to a hosting provider issue. We were alerted within two minutes and the site recovered automatically.
>
> **Upcoming renewals:** The SSL certificate for booking.example.com renews by 12 October. We are tracking it.
>
> **Live status:** status page link

## How often to send it

Monthly, on the first working day of the month. Weekly is noise; quarterly is too infrequent to build the habit of seeing your name.

## Automate it

Compiling reports by hand from monitoring dashboards takes an hour per client per month — which is why most agencies stop doing it after three months. Use a monitoring tool that generates and emails the report automatically, branded with your agency's name and logo rather than the tool's. Then the report keeps going out every month whether or not you remember, and every one of them reminds the client why they hired you.

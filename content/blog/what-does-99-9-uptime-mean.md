Hosting companies and monitoring tools love to quote uptime percentages, but “99.9%” is hard to feel. Here is what each common figure means in actual downtime.

## Uptime percentages in minutes and hours

Based on an average month of 30.4 days and a year of 365 days:

| Uptime | Downtime per month | Downtime per year |
|---|---|---|
| 99% | about 7.3 hours | about 3.65 days |
| 99.5% | about 3.65 hours | about 1.8 days |
| 99.9% | about 44 minutes | about 8.8 hours |
| 99.95% | about 22 minutes | about 4.4 hours |
| 99.99% | about 4.4 minutes | about 53 minutes |

The jump from 99% to 99.9% is the difference between a working day of downtime every month and less than an hour.

## What a small business website should aim for

For most small business websites on decent hosting, **99.9% or better** over a month is a realistic target. A month below 99.5% usually points to a specific problem worth fixing: an overloaded server, a plugin that crashes under load, or a host with recurring incidents.

## Why measured uptime can differ from your host's figure

- **Your host measures its servers; you care about your website.** A server can be up while the site shows a database error.
- **Planned maintenance** is often excluded from a host's guarantee but still counts for your visitors.
- **Expired certificates and domains** are not hosting outages at all — yet to a visitor the site is just as gone.

That is why it is worth monitoring from the outside, the way a visitor experiences the site, including keyword checks for pages that load but are broken.

## How uptime is calculated

Monitoring tools check the site at regular intervals and divide successful checks by total checks. With one-minute checks there are around 43,800 per month, so a single failed check barely moves the number — which is why good tools confirm a failure with a re-check before declaring the site down.

## Using uptime with clients

A monthly uptime figure is one of the clearest numbers you can put in front of a client. Combined with response time and a list of incidents handled, it turns “we look after your website” into something concrete they can see every month.

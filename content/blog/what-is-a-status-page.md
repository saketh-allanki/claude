A status page is a simple public web page that answers two questions: **is it working right now?** and **how reliable has it been?** Large software companies have had them for years. They are just as useful for small businesses and the agencies that look after their websites.

## What a status page shows

- **Current status** of each website or service: operational, degraded or down.
- **Uptime history** — usually a bar per day for the last 90 days.
- **Recent incidents** with when they started and how long they lasted.

The best status pages update automatically from monitoring, so nobody has to remember to change them during an outage.

## Why a small business benefits

### Fewer “is the website down?” messages

When something breaks, customers and staff want to know whether it is them or you. A status page answers that instantly, without a phone call.

### Visible reliability

A long row of green bars is a quiet, persuasive signal that the business takes its online presence seriously — useful for anyone selling online or taking bookings.

### A record for conversations with the host

When you need to raise reliability with a hosting provider, a 90-day uptime history turns “it feels like the site goes down a lot” into a specific, dated list.

## Why agencies benefit even more

For agencies, a status page per client is a low-effort way to show the value of a maintenance plan. The client can bookmark it, link to it from their website footer, and see at a glance that someone is watching. Combined with a short monthly report, it turns an invisible service into a visible one.

## What to put on it

- One line per customer-facing service (website, shop, booking system, portal) — not every server.
- Human names (“Online booking”), not hostnames.
- Your branding or the client's, so it feels like part of their website.

## Where to link it

- In the website footer (“System status”).
- In the email signature of the support team.
- In client onboarding documents and monthly reports.
- As a small live **status badge** on the website or in documentation.

## Do you need one?

If customers rely on a website to buy, book or log in, yes — and it costs almost nothing if your monitoring tool generates it automatically. If you look after websites for clients, a branded status page per client is one of the simplest ways to make your work visible.
